\begin{abstract}
Marine heatwaves are increasingly associated with declines in fished
populations, and catch per unit effort (CPUE) is the signal most often
used to detect them. CPUE is, however, the product of three processes:
abundance, availability (the spatial overlap of the stock with the
fishery) and catchability (the efficiency with which available fish are
caught). A marine heatwave can perturb each of these, so a CPUE based
model that carries a single heatwave term estimates their sum rather
than a population response. Whether that term can be attributed to
abundance is usually assumed rather than tested, and it cannot be tested
empirically because the true decomposition of a real catch rate is never
observed. We address the question by simulation, where the truth is
known by construction. We first show that total abundance and spatially
uniform catchability are structurally non identifiable from fishery data
alone, that spatially structured catchability is identified, and that a
survey with protocol fixed catchability breaks the degeneracy. We then
build a mechanistic operating model in which abundance, availability and
catchability all respond to one driver, the species' thermal niche, with
an open poleward boundary beyond which a refuge warms with the frame,
removal based fishing mortality, tow level observation of catch under
preferential effort, and an optional fishery independent survey. Two
estimands are defined by paired counterfactuals: the event effect on
total abundance and on in frame abundance. Over a \(2^{4}\) factorial of
decline, redistribution, uniform catchability loss and fleet
contraction, with 400 replicates per cell, no fishery index was robust
to uniform catchability change, and nothing in the fishery data can make
one so; a spatially explicit delta model did recover in frame abundance
under redistribution, so availability has a fishery only remedy that
catchability lacks. The survey index was unbiased for in frame abundance
in every cell and biased for total abundance by exactly the share of the
stock that an event moves across the frame boundary, which no
observation confined to the frame can see. A spatial centre of gravity
diagnostic recovered redistribution where the scalar coefficient was
blind, although a warm edge catchability change imitated that shift in
the fishery data and was separated from genuine movement only by the
centre of gravity of an independent survey. Survey frequency mattered
more than station count: a monthly survey of 7 to 14 stations
outperformed every fishery estimator, a quarterly survey needed several
times the stations, and a semiannual survey could not separate the event
from the seasonal cycle. The framework specifies which spatial
structure, auxiliary data and temporal structure resolve each component
of a heatwave signal, and how much of each is needed, converting an
inferential impossibility into a monitoring design problem with
quantitative answers.

\textbf{Keywords:} marine heatwave; catch per unit effort; catchability;
availability; hyperstability; identifiability; operating model; species
distribution; simulation; survey design
\end{abstract}

\hypertarget{introduction}{%
\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}}

Marine heatwaves, prolonged and discrete periods of anomalously warm
water, have become a defining feature of contemporary ocean change
(Hobday et al., 2016). They have lengthened and intensified over the
past century (Oliver et al., 2018), are projected to become more
frequent and severe under continued warming (Frölicher et al., 2018;
Afrifa-Yamoah et al., 2026) and now rank among the most acute threats to
marine biodiversity and the ecosystem services it underpins (Smale et
al., 2019). Their biological consequences are large and varied, ranging
from mass mortality and habitat loss to wholesale community
reorganisation and shifts in species distributions (Wernberg et al.,
2013, 2016; Babcock et al., 2019; Cheung and Frölicher, 2020). For
fished populations in particular, the pressing management question is
quantitative: by how much did a given heatwave change the resource, and
how confident can we be in that number?

The datum most often brought to bear on that question is catch per unit
effort. It is collected continuously by the fishery itself, extends back
decades and covers the fished range at fine spatial and temporal
resolution, so a fall in CPUE that coincides with a heatwave is a
natural candidate for a population signal. The difficulty is that CPUE
is not a measurement of abundance. It is the joint outcome of three
distinct processes. Abundance is the size of the population.
Availability is the fraction of that population that overlaps the
fishery in space and time, and therefore could in principle be caught.
Catchability is the efficiency with which available fish are actually
taken, given the gear, the behaviour of the fish and the way effort is
deployed. Writing catch rate schematically as the product of
catchability, availability and abundance makes the identification
problem plain: a heatwave can move all three at once, and a model that
summarises the event with a single coefficient recovers a combination of
the three responses, not the abundance response alone.

This is not a subtle bias to be bounded and set aside. The three
responses need not even share a sign. A heatwave can reduce abundance
through mortality or recruitment failure; it can raise or lower
availability by redistributing the stock into or out of the fished area;
and it can raise or lower catchability by concentrating fish into
catchable aggregations or dispersing them out of them. Hyperstability,
in which CPUE remains high while abundance falls because the fishery
tracks a contracting distribution, is the best known manifestation and
has been documented across many fisheries (Harley et al., 2001; Erisman
et al., 2011), and the more general difficulty of reading stock status
from catch rates is long recognised (Maunder et al., 2006). The tendency
of many stocks to contract their occupied area as they decline, which
drives this behaviour, is itself well documented (Thorson et al.,
2016b). Each of these pathways has ample precedent in the heatwave and
climate literature, but the field lacks a framework that treats them
jointly and asks what, if anything, a CPUE based attribution can recover
when all three operate together.

Standardisation is the usual response to the fact that nominal CPUE is
confounded. Statistical standardisation, which conditions catch rate on
measurable covariates of the fishing operation, is now routine and is
supported by a mature set of good practices for stock assessment inputs
(Maunder and Punt, 2004; Hoyle et al., 2024). Spatiotemporal and
geostatistical index standardisation has advanced the state of the art
considerably by modelling the spatial field explicitly (Thorson et al.,
2015; Thorson and Barnett, 2017; Thorson, 2019), is increasingly
recommended for deriving abundance indices from catch rates (Maunder et
al., 2020; Thorson et al., 2020), and can additionally yield model based
estimates of distribution shift such as the centre of gravity (Thorson
et al., 2016a). Simulation work has clarified how sampling design and
spatial dependence propagate into survey and CPUE based indices
(Fuster-Alonso et al., 2024). Even so, it has long been argued that a
standardised index in isolation gives limited information about the
status of an individual stock (Maunder et al., 2006). These methods
condition out the part of the variation that is explained by observed
covariates and by spatial position. What they cannot do is separate
abundance from availability and catchability when the three move
together in response to an unobserved common driver, because no
covariate of the fishing operation measures the latent thermal response
of the stock. That separation is a question of identifiability, not of
estimator refinement, and it is the question we take up here.

We make the argument by simulation because the object of interest, the
decomposition of a realised catch rate into its three multiplicative
parts, is never observed in a real fishery. Simulation makes it
observable by construction. We build an operating model in which one
environmental driver, the species' thermal niche, generates all three
responses simultaneously, observe catch through a realistic sampling
process that is itself perturbed by the heatwave, apply the same
standardisation tools that would be used on real data and compare each
recovered signal against the mechanism that produced it. The
contribution is threefold. First, we establish that the central obstacle
is structural: total abundance and uniform catchability are non
identifiable from fishery data alone, as a matter of the likelihood
rather than of finite sample noise, while spatially structured
catchability is identified. Second, we show that the survey which breaks
that degeneracy is unbiased for the abundance inside its frame and not
for the abundance of the stock, and that the distinction is set by
whether the event moves biomass across the frame boundary, which makes
the choice of estimand part of the design. Third, we turn the negative
result into a constructive one by identifying, for each confounding
pathway, the spatial structure, auxiliary data or temporal structure
that resolves it, and by quantifying how much of each is needed,
including how often a survey must sample. The result reframes heatwave
attribution from CPUE as a monitoring design problem with quantitative
answers rather than an inferential dead end.

\hypertarget{a-structural-view-of-the-problem}{%
\section{A structural view of the
problem}\label{a-structural-view-of-the-problem}}

\hypertarget{the-observation-model-and-its-invariance}{%
\subsection{The observation model and its
invariance}\label{the-observation-model-and-its-invariance}}

Let the fished domain be a lattice of \(S\) cells indexed by \(s\) and
months indexed by \(t\). Write \(N_t\) for total abundance, \(\pi_{st}\)
for the share of the population in cell \(s\), \(q_{st}\) for
catchability and \(E_{st}\) for fishing effort. The population shares
satisfy the frame constraint
\(\sum_s \pi_{st} + \pi^{\text{out}}_t = 1\), where
\(\pi^{\text{out}}_t\) is the fraction outside the sampling frame. The
expected catch rate in a fished cell is

\begin{equation}
\mu_{st} = q_{st}\,E_{st}\,N_t\,\pi_{st}.
\end{equation}

\noindent Every fishery observation, whether an encounter, a positive catch or a
standardised index, is a function of the state only through
\(\mu_{st}\). This is the crux of the identification problem. For any
strictly positive monthly sequence \(a_t\), the reparameterisation

\begin{equation}
N_t^{\star} = a_t\,N_t, \qquad q_{st}^{\star} = q_{st} / a_t
\end{equation}

\noindent leaves \(\mu_{st}\) unchanged in every cell and every month, so that the
encounter probability, the positive catch distribution, the effort
allocation and hence the entire fishery likelihood satisfy
\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{fish}}(N_{1:T}, q) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{fish}}(N_{1:T}^{\star}, q^{\star})\).
Total abundance and any spatially uniform component of catchability are
therefore not separately identified from fishery data alone: they are
confounded exactly, not approximately. No amount of data and no
refinement of the standardisation model can break an invariance of the
likelihood itself. A fishery independent survey observes
\(q^{\text{surv}}_{st} N_t \pi_{st}\) with a catchability that does not
carry the factor \(a_t\), so it is not invariant under the same
reparameterisation and breaks the degeneracy. We verify this claim
numerically in Section 4.1, where the fishery only information matrix
\(\mathcal{I}_{\text{fish}}(\beta_N, \beta_q)\) for the abundance and
catchability slopes is singular to machine precision and the combined
information matrix
\(\mathcal{I}_{\text{fish}} + \mathcal{I}_{\text{surv}}\) is well
conditioned.

Two clarifications matter for what follows. The invariance is a
statement about the uniform (domain wide) part of catchability.
Spatially structured catchability, for instance a change confined to the
warm edge of the range, is not annihilated by a scalar \emph{a}\(_{t}\)
and interacts with the spatial machinery of the estimators in more
subtle ways, which is why we treat it as a separate mechanism. And
structural non identifiability, a flat direction in the likelihood, is
distinct from practical non identifiability, a likelihood that is curved
but nearly flat at realistic sample sizes. We keep the two separate
throughout: Section 4.1 establishes the structural result, and the
finite sample experiments that follow quantify how the practical problem
behaves once the structural one is granted.

\hypertarget{the-mechanisms-as-an-experimental-design}{%
\subsection{The mechanisms as an experimental
design}\label{the-mechanisms-as-an-experimental-design}}

The operating model lets each response be switched on or off
independently. Three are biological or operational channels through
which a heatwave can change a catch rate: abundance decline,
redistribution or availability change, and uniform catchability loss. A
fourth is a fleet response: an event driven contraction of the fished
footprint, in which the fleet concentrates on the cells it knows best.
Together they give a full \(2^{4}\) factorial of sixteen cells. We treat
these as the factors of a designed experiment rather than as a handful
of illustrative scenarios, which lets us estimate not only the main
effect of each mechanism on each estimator but also the interactions
among them, including the interaction between the fleet's contraction
and the biological channels, which is where a sampling artefact can
appear. We do not assume the mechanisms combine additively on the
coefficient scale; we estimate the interaction terms and report them
(Section 4.2). To this core design we add four spatially structured
catchability cells, in which catchability declines only at the warm edge
of the range, with and without redistribution and with and without the
fleet contraction, to test whether such a change can imitate genuine
redistribution.

\hypertarget{two-estimands}{%
\subsection{Two estimands}\label{two-estimands}}

Throughout, the targets of estimation are event attributable effects
defined by a paired counterfactual. Every replicate is generated twice
under common random numbers: once with the heatwave series as drawn,
giving the factual log abundance \(\log N_t\) and the share of the stock
inside the sampling frame, and once with the event anomaly removed while
climatology, the chronic warming trend, the noise field, the process
noise, the fleet and the realised effort allocation are all held fixed,
giving the counterfactual \(\log N^{0}_t\) and its frame share. The
effect on total abundance is \(\Delta_t = \log N_t - \log N^{0}_t\); the
effect on in frame abundance, \(\Delta^{\text{in}}_t\), adds the change
in the log frame share. The scalar estimands \(\beta\) and
\(\beta^{\text{in}}\) are the partial slopes of these series on centred
severity \(I_t\), controlling for a linear trend and a first order
seasonal harmonic,

\begin{equation}
\Delta_t = \alpha + \beta\,I_t + \zeta_1\,\tfrac{t}{T} + \zeta_2 \sin\!\tfrac{2\pi t}{12} + \zeta_3 \cos\!\tfrac{2\pi t}{12} + u_t.
\end{equation}

\noindent We carry both because a fishery independent survey of the frame can be
unbiased only for the second: when an event moves biomass across the
frame boundary, in frame abundance and total abundance diverge, and no
observation confined to the frame can tell them apart. Which of the two
a manager needs depends on whether the stock beyond the boundary is
still the stock being managed. Defining the estimands as within
replicate contrasts, rather than as marginal associations, is what
separates the event effect from background warming and seasonality, and
the identical specification is applied to every estimator so that all of
them target the same quantities. In the parameterisation used here the
marginal association of factual log abundance with severity agrees with
\(\beta\) to within 0.002, which we report so that the reader can see
that the distinction, although real, is not what drives the results
below.

\hypertarget{methods}{%
\section{Methods}\label{methods}}

\hypertarget{model-equations}{%
\subsection{Model equations}\label{model-equations}}

This section states the operating model and estimators formally; the
subsections that follow describe the same components in prose and give
the parameter values used. The state variables are total abundance
\(N_t\), the distribution shares \(\pi_{st}\), catchability \(q_{st}\)
and the temperature field \(T_{st}\); the observed quantities are the
catch rate \(Y_{st}\) and the survey catch \(V_{st}\).

\hypertarget{domain-and-notation}{%
\subsubsection{Domain and notation}\label{domain-and-notation}}

The fished domain is a regular lattice of
\(S = n_{\text{lat}} \times n_{\text{shelf}}\) cells indexed by
\(s = 1, \dots, S\), observed over \(t = 1, \dots, T\) monthly time
steps. Each cell has a latitude band \(\ell(s)\), a cross shelf band
\(c(s)\), and standardised spatial coordinates \(y_s\) (northing) and
\(x_s\) (offshore position). Total abundance in month \(t\) is \(N_t\),
and the population is distributed across cells by the shares
\(\pi_{st} \ge 0\), which satisfy the frame constraint

\begin{equation}
\sum_{s=1}^{S} \pi_{st} + \pi^{\text{out}}_t = 1,
\end{equation}

\noindent where \(\pi^{\text{out}}_t\) is the fraction of the population outside
the sampling frame. Catchability is \(q_{st}\), effort is \(E_{st}\),
and the event severity in month \(t\) is \(J_t \ge 0\).

\hypertarget{temperature-field}{%
\subsubsection{Temperature field}\label{temperature-field}}

Temperature is the sum of a deterministic baseline and the heatwave
anomaly, with the baseline itself decomposed so that the counterfactual
is well defined:

\begin{equation}
T_{st} = \underbrace{\bar{T} - g_{\text{lat}}\,\ell(s) - g_{\text{dep}}\,c(s)}_{\text{climatology}} + \underbrace{A\sin\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi t}{12} - \phi\right)}_{\text{season}} + \underbrace{\gamma\,\tfrac{t}{12}}_{\text{trend}} + \underbrace{\varepsilon_{st}}_{\text{noise}} + \underbrace{H_{st}}_{\text{event}} .
\end{equation}

\noindent The noise field \(\varepsilon_{st}\) is separable, first order
autoregressive in both space and time, with marginal standard deviation
\(\sigma_{\varepsilon}\), nearest neighbour spatial correlation
\(\rho_s\), and temporal correlation \(\rho_t\). The event anomaly
\(H_{st}\) is non negative and is set to zero in the counterfactual
world. The baseline temperature, obtained by deleting \(H_{st}\), is
written \(T^{0}_{st}\).

\hypertarget{heatwave-severity}{%
\subsubsection{Heatwave severity}\label{heatwave-severity}}

Events are placed on a schedule of \(K\) episodes with random start
months, durations, and peak anomalies. Within an event the anomaly
follows a smooth rise and decay. The monthly severity is the category
weighted intensity above the category one threshold \(\tau\),

\begin{equation}
J_t = \frac{\max\!\left(H_t - \tau,\ 0\right)}{\tau}, \qquad H_t = \frac{1}{S}\sum_{s} H_{st},
\end{equation}

\noindent so \(J_t \ge 0\) by construction. Every biological and fleet response is
driven by \(J_t\). For estimation, and only for estimation, the centred
and scaled covariate

\begin{equation}
I_t = \frac{J_t - \bar{J}}{\operatorname{sd}(J)}
\end{equation}

\noindent is used, so that no estimator receives the sign of the true response
through its covariate.

\hypertarget{thermal-niche-movement-and-the-refuge-beyond-the-frame}{%
\subsubsection{Thermal niche, movement, and the refuge beyond the
frame}\label{thermal-niche-movement-and-the-refuge-beyond-the-frame}}

Thermal suitability is Gaussian in the distance between local
temperature and the optimum \(T_{\text{opt}}\),

\begin{equation}
\phi_{st} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(T_{st} - T_{\text{opt}})^2}{2\,\sigma_{\text{th}}^2}\right).
\end{equation}

\noindent The sampling frame is open at its poleward edge. A refuge state one
latitude band beyond the edge has temperature
\(T^{\text{ref}}_t = \bar{T} - g_{\text{lat}}\,(n_{\text{lat}} + 1 - \bar{\ell}) + A\sin(2\pi t/12 - \phi) + \gamma\,t/12 + H_t\),
the same climatological gradient, season, trend and event anomaly that
the frame experiences, and suitability \(\phi^{\text{ref}}_t\) given by
the same niche function. It carries the area weight of one band,
\(w^{\text{ref}} = n_{\text{shelf}}\). When the redistribution mechanism
is active the population tracks an ideal free target, subject to partial
adjustment: with
\(\tilde{\phi}_{st} = \phi_{st} / \big(\sum_{s'} \phi_{s't} + w^{\text{ref}}\phi^{\text{ref}}_t\big)\)
and \(\tilde{\phi}^{\text{ref}}_t\) defined likewise,

\begin{equation}
\pi_{st} = (1 - \rho_m)\,\pi_{s,t-1} + \rho_m\,\tilde{\phi}_{st}, \qquad \pi^{\text{out}}_t = (1 - \rho_m)\,\pi^{\text{out}}_{t-1} + \rho_m\,\tilde{\phi}^{\text{ref}}_t,
\end{equation}

\noindent renormalised each month, with adjustment rate \(\rho_m \in (0,1)\).
Because the refuge warms with the frame, biomass leaves the frame only
when conditions beyond the edge are better than within it, which in this
parameterisation occurs in warm seasons and during events. A refuge with
time invariant suitability, the specification used in an earlier version
of this work, is retained as a sensitivity comparison (Section 4.5): it
behaves as a thermostat that draws biomass out of the frame regardless
of conditions. When redistribution is inactive the shares are held at
their climatological values and \(\pi^{\text{out}}_t\) is constant.

\hypertarget{abundance-state-process}{%
\subsubsection{Abundance state process}\label{abundance-state-process}}

Abundance follows a discrete transition on the log scale with density
dependent recovery, removal based fishing mortality, and, when the
decline mechanism is active, a response to a lagged thermal dose:

\begin{equation}
\log N_t = \log N_{t-1} + r_0\!\left(1 - \frac{N_{t-1}}{K_{\text{cap}}}\right) - M - F_t - \kappa\,d_{t-L} + \eta_t,
\end{equation}

\noindent where \(r_0\) is the intrinsic recovery rate, \(K_{\text{cap}}\) the
carrying capacity, \(M\) natural mortality, \(\kappa\) the abundance
sensitivity, \(L\) the response lag, and
\(\eta_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_{\text{proc}}^2)\) process noise.
Fishing mortality is proportional to the expected catch per unit
abundance implied by the month's effort allocation,

\begin{equation}
F_t = f\,S\sum_{s=1}^{S} q_{st}\,\pi_{st}\,E_{st} \ \ge 0,
\end{equation}

\noindent so it rises with the overlap between fleet and stock and falls when
catchability falls. The thermal dose is occupancy weighted, so that only
the temperature the stock actually experiences drives the decline:

\begin{equation}
d_t = \sum_{s=1}^{S} \pi_{st}\,\max\!\left(T_{st} - T_{\text{crit}},\ 0\right).
\end{equation}

\hypertarget{catchability}{%
\subsubsection{Catchability}\label{catchability}}

Catchability combines a spatially uniform component, structurally
confounded with abundance, and a spatially structured warm edge
component:

\begin{equation}
q_{st} = q_0 \cdot \underbrace{\exp\!\left(-\delta_{\text{dep}}\,\hat{J}_t\right)}_{\text{uniform}} \cdot \underbrace{\exp\!\left(-\delta_{\text{sp}}\,g_s\,\hat{J}_t\right)}_{\text{warm edge}},
\end{equation}

\noindent where \(\hat{J}_t\) is the severity scaled to unit standard deviation
over event months (and identically zero in a world without events) and
\(g_s = h_s / \bar{h}\),
\(h_s = \max(1 - \gamma_{\text{sp}}\,y_s,\ 0)\), is a latitudinal weight
normalised to domain mean one, so the warm edge term redistributes
catchability without changing its average level. Each component is
switched on or off independently.

\hypertarget{observation-model}{%
\subsubsection{Observation model}\label{observation-model}}

The latent catch rate per unit effort in cell \(s\) is

\begin{equation}
\mu_{st} = S\,q_{st}\,N_t\,\pi_{st},
\end{equation}

\noindent where the factor \(S\) expresses the share \(\pi_{st}\) as a density
relative to the domain mean. A cell receiving \(E_{st}\) effort units is
observed through \(E_{st}\) tows. The cell encounters the stock if at
least one tow does,

\begin{equation}
p_{st} = 1 - \exp\!\left(-a_0\,\mu_{st}\,E_{st}\right),
\end{equation}

\noindent and the cell's catch rate, the mean over its tows, is lognormal given an
encounter with variance that falls with effort, parameterised so that
the two parts are mean coherent at any effort,
\(\mathbb{E}[Y_{st}] = \mu_{st}\):

\begin{equation}
Y_{st} \mid (\text{encounter}) \sim \operatorname{Lognormal}\!\left(\log\frac{\mu_{st}}{p_{st}} - \tfrac{1}{2}\,\frac{\sigma_{\log}^2}{E_{st}},\ \frac{\sigma_{\log}^2}{E_{st}}\right), \qquad \Pr(Y_{st} = 0) = 1 - p_{st}.
\end{equation}

\noindent The coherence follows because
\(\mathbb{E}[Y_{st}] = p_{st}\cdot(\mu_{st}/p_{st}) = \mu_{st}\). Catch
is drawn from the abundance \(N_t\) of the same month, after that
month's removals, so the observations and the truth refer to one state.

\hypertarget{effort-allocation}{%
\subsubsection{Effort allocation}\label{effort-allocation}}

Total monthly effort \(E_{\text{tot}}\) is allocated across cells by a
multinomial draw on information available before month \(t\):

\begin{equation}
\left(E_{1t}, \dots, E_{St}\right) \sim \operatorname{Multinomial}\!\left(E_{\text{tot}},\ w_{\cdot t}\right), \qquad w_{st} = \frac{\exp(\lambda_{st})}{\sum_{s'} \exp(\lambda_{s't})},
\end{equation}

\noindent with the allocation score

\begin{equation}
\lambda_{st} = \beta_\mu\left(1 + \beta_J\,\hat{J}_t\,\mathbb{1}\{\text{contraction}\}\right) b_{s,t-1} - \beta_{\text{dist}}\,\text{dist}_s + \nu_{v(s)},
\end{equation}

\noindent where \(b_{s,t-1}\) is the fleet's standardised belief about cell catch
rates, updated each month as a blend of its previous belief and the last
observed log catch rate (carried forward where a cell went unfished),
\(\text{dist}_s\) the travel cost from port and \(\nu_{v(s)}\) a vessel
level efficiency effect. When the contraction mechanism is active an
event sharpens the fleet's targeting, so effort concentrates on fewer,
better known cells and the fished footprint shrinks. The contraction
must enter as an interaction with targeting: a term added to every
cell's score cancels in the softmax and has no effect at all, which is
how an earlier version of this model rendered the mechanism inert.
Because effort magnitude is generated, catch rate is \(Y_{st}\) (catch
per unit effort) throughout rather than a latent density.

\hypertarget{survey}{%
\subsubsection{Survey}\label{survey}}

The fishery independent survey samples \(n = \lceil c\,S \rceil\) cells
without replacement in every \(k\)th month (\(k = 1\) monthly, 3
quarterly, 6 semiannual), either by simple random sampling or with equal
allocation across latitude bands. Both designs give equal inclusion
probability \(p^{\text{inc}} = n / S\); stratification changes the
variance of the estimator, not its form. Survey observations follow
their own delta lognormal model with catchability \(q^{\text{surv}}\)
and encounter scale \(a^{\text{surv}}\) fixed by protocol, neither of
which responds to the event. The design based index is a
Horvitz--Thompson total and the centroid is a Hájek ratio,

\begin{equation}
\widehat{B}_t = \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_t} \frac{V_{st}}{p^{\text{inc}}}, \qquad \widehat{G}_t = \frac{\sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_t} y_s\,V_{st}}{\sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}_t} V_{st}},
\end{equation}

\noindent where \(\mathcal{S}_t\) is the sampled set and \(V_{st}\) the survey
catch. The total estimates in frame abundance,
\(S\,q^{\text{surv}} N_t\,(1 - \pi^{\text{out}}_t)\), not total
abundance.

\hypertarget{structural-non-identifiability}{%
\subsubsection{Structural non
identifiability}\label{structural-non-identifiability}}

Every fishery observation depends on the state only through
\(\mu_{st} = S\,q_{st} N_t \pi_{st}\). For any strictly positive
sequence \(a_t\), the reparameterisation

\begin{equation}
N_t^{\star} = a_t\,N_t, \qquad q_{st}^{\star} = \frac{q_{st}}{a_t}
\end{equation}

\noindent leaves \(\mu_{st}\) unchanged for every \(s\) and \(t\), and hence
leaves the encounter probability, the positive catch density, the effort
allocation (which responds to observed catch rates), the removals (which
depend on \(q\,\pi\,E\)), and the entire fishery likelihood
\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{fish}}\) invariant:

\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{fish}}\!\left(N_{1:T}, q_{1:S,1:T}\right) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{fish}}\!\left(N_{1:T}^{\star}, q_{1:S,1:T}^{\star}\right).
\end{equation}

\noindent Total abundance and any spatially uniform component of catchability are
therefore not separately identified from fishery data alone. The survey
observes \(S\,q^{\text{surv}} N_t \pi_{st}\) with a catchability that
does not carry \(a_t\), so it is not invariant under the same
reparameterisation and breaks the degeneracy. The spatially structured
component \(\delta_{\text{sp}} g_s \hat{J}_t\) is not annihilated by a
scalar \(a_t\) and is identified from fishery data alone. Writing
\(\beta_N\), \(\beta_q\) and \(\gamma\) for the log linear responses of
abundance, uniform catchability and warm edge catchability to severity,
the observed information
\(\mathcal{I}_{\text{fish}}(\beta_N, \beta_q, \gamma)\) has exactly one
zero eigenvalue, in the \((\beta_N, \beta_q)\) direction, while
\(\mathcal{I}_{\text{fish}} + \mathcal{I}_{\text{surv}}\) is of full
rank. Section 4.1 illustrates this on a reduced likelihood; the
illustration follows from the functional form above and is not an
independent test of it.

\hypertarget{estimands}{%
\subsubsection{Estimands}\label{estimands}}

Two targets are carried, because they answer different questions. Every
replicate is generated twice under common random numbers: once with the
event anomaly as drawn, giving the factual log abundance \(\log N_t\)
and frame share \(1 - \pi^{\text{out}}_t\), and once with the anomaly
deleted while climatology, trend, noise field, process noise, fleet and
the realised effort allocation are held fixed, giving \(\log N^{0}_t\)
and \(1 - \pi^{\text{out},0}_t\). The event attributable effect on total
abundance and on in frame abundance are

\begin{equation}
\Delta_t = \log N_t - \log N^{0}_t, \qquad \Delta^{\text{in}}_t = \Delta_t + \log\!\big(1 - \pi^{\text{out}}_t\big) - \log\!\big(1 - \pi^{\text{out},0}_t\big),
\end{equation}

\noindent and the scalar estimands \(\beta\) and \(\beta^{\text{in}}\) are the
partial slopes of \(\Delta_t\) and \(\Delta^{\text{in}}_t\) on centred
severity \(I_t\), controlling for a linear trend and a first order
seasonal harmonic:

\begin{equation}
\Delta_t = \alpha + \beta\,I_t + \zeta_1\,\tfrac{t}{T} + \zeta_2 \sin\!\tfrac{2\pi t}{12} + \zeta_3 \cos\!\tfrac{2\pi t}{12} + u_t.
\end{equation}

\noindent The two coincide whenever the event does not move biomass across the
frame boundary. Holding the realised allocation fixed makes \(\Delta_t\)
a direct effect of the event at the fleet's factual behaviour; removals
still differ between the two worlds through \(q\) and \(\pi\). The
identical regression is applied to every estimated index, with a
Newey--West standard error (Bartlett kernel, three lags) on the severity
coefficient so that confidence interval coverage and power can be
reported.

\hypertarget{index-estimators}{%
\subsubsection{Index estimators}\label{index-estimators}}

Each estimator produces a monthly log index to which the estimand
regression above is applied. The nominal index is total catch over total
effort,

\begin{equation}
\widehat{I}^{\text{nom}}_t = \log \frac{\sum_s Y_{st} E_{st}}{\sum_s E_{st}\,\mathbb{1}\{Y_{st}\ \text{observed}\}} .
\end{equation}

\noindent The delta two way index fits additive cell and month effects to the
encounter and positive components,

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{logit} p_{st} = a^{p}_s + b^{p}_t, \qquad \log Y_{st} \mid (Y_{st} > 0) = \mu^{m} + a^{m}_s + b^{m}_t + e_{st},
\end{equation}

\noindent with a light ridge on the cell effects of the encounter component,
because cells that are never encountered have no finite maximum
likelihood estimate. It predicts on the complete reference grid at fixed
effort,

\begin{equation}
\widehat{I}^{\delta}_t = \log\!\left(\frac{1}{S}\sum_{s=1}^{S} \hat{p}_{st}\,\exp\!\big(\hat{\mu}^m + \hat{a}^m_s + \hat{b}^m_t + \tfrac{1}{2}\hat{\sigma}^2_m\big)\right).
\end{equation}

\noindent Cells not fished in a month receive the model's prediction, an
imputation whose share is reported; a variant that averages only over
the cells fished in that month is reported alongside it. The delta
interaction index adds a low dimensional space by severity term
\(\mathbf{z}_s^{\top}\boldsymbol{\theta}\,I_t\), with
\(\mathbf{z}_s = (y_s, x_s)\), to each component, producing a
standardised density surface on fixed support that can move its own
centre of gravity. An integrated fishery plus survey index was fitted in
an earlier version; it shared a month effect across streams, so the
fishery's catchability change loaded directly onto the shared effect,
and it is not reported here (Section 5).

\hypertarget{operating-model}{%
\subsection{Operating model}\label{operating-model}}

The state process unfolds on a lattice of twelve latitude bands by
twelve cross shelf bands, each cell 50 km on a side, over 96 monthly
time steps, and the factual world is simulated in a single sequential
loop: effort in a month is allocated from the fleet's belief at the end
of the previous month, abundance in that month carries the removals
implied by that effort, and catch is drawn from that abundance. The
counterfactual world re-runs the state process without the event
anomaly, holding the realised allocation and all noise fixed.
Temperature in each cell and month is the sum of four components kept
deliberately separate: a spatial climatology that cools with latitude
and with distance offshore; a seasonal cycle; a chronic warming trend of
0.05 °C per year; and a spatiotemporally correlated noise field with
separable spatial and temporal autocorrelation. Onto this baseline we
add the heatwave anomaly. Keeping the deterministic climatology, the
trend and the event anomaly as distinct terms is what makes the paired
counterfactual of Section 2.3 well defined: the counterfactual world is
obtained by deleting the event term alone.

Heatwaves are generated by an explicit schedule. Six events per
replicate are drawn at random start months (overlapping events merge,
and the realised count is recorded) with durations drawn between one and
four months and peak anomalies drawn around 2.2 °C. Within each event
the anomaly follows a smooth rise and decay. The monthly event severity
\(J_t\) is the category weighted intensity above the category one
threshold, and it is by construction non negative: it drives every
biological and fleet response in the model. For estimation only, we use
the centred and scaled covariate \(I_t\), so that no estimator is handed
the sign of the true response through the covariate.

Abundance follows a discrete state transition on the log scale, with a
density dependent recovery term, a removal based fishing mortality
proportional to the expected catch per unit abundance implied by the
month's effort allocation (so that it is non negative, rises with fleet
stock overlap and falls when catchability falls), and, when the decline
mechanism is active, a response to a lagged thermal dose. The thermal
dose is occupancy weighted: it is the sum over cells of the population
share in each cell times the excess of temperature over a stress
threshold, so that only the temperature actually experienced by the
stock, not the domain average, drives the decline. Movement follows a
partial adjustment process toward an ideal free target set by the
thermal suitability surface, with an explicit refuge state one latitude
band beyond the poleward edge of the sampling domain. The refuge
experiences the same seasonal cycle, warming trend and event anomaly as
the frame, so biomass leaves the frame only when conditions beyond the
edge are better than within it. The frame is open by default and closed
in a sensitivity comparison, and a refuge with fixed suitability, which
behaves as a thermostat, is retained as a second comparison; as Section
4.5 shows, the geometry of the frame and the refuge materially changes
how redistribution registers in CPUE and in a survey. Suitability is a
Gaussian function of the distance between local temperature and the
thermal optimum.

Catchability combines a uniform component and a spatial component. The
uniform component multiplies catchability by a factor that declines with
event severity, and is the piece that is structurally confounded with
abundance. The spatial component, used only in the warm edge cells,
reduces catchability in proportion to a normalised latitudinal weight
whose domain mean is one, so that the spatial mechanism redistributes
catchability across the domain without changing its average level.

\hypertarget{observation-model-1}{%
\subsection{Observation model}\label{observation-model-1}}

Catch is observed tow by tow through a delta lognormal process with two
parts, an encounter probability and a positive catch distribution, so
that the zeros that dominate real catch records enter the index directly
rather than being discarded. A cell that receives more effort is
observed through more tows: its probability of at least one encounter
rises and the variance of its mean catch rate falls. We parameterise the
two parts coherently so that the unconditional mean of the observation
equals the latent catch rate at any effort: the positive component is
centred on the catch rate divided by the encounter probability, so that
the encounter probability times the conditional mean returns
\(\mu_{st}\). This removes an inconsistency that would otherwise bias
every index that passes through the delta model.

Fishing effort is generated, not assumed. Total monthly effort is fixed
and allocated across cells by a multinomial draw whose probabilities
depend on information available before the current month: the catch rate
observed in each cell in the previous month, carried forward where a
cell went unfished; a travel cost that penalises distance from port; and
vessel level efficiency differences. When the contraction mechanism is
active, an event sharpens the fleet's targeting of the cells it believes
to be best, so effort concentrates on fewer cells during heatwaves and
the fished footprint shrinks. Because effort magnitude is generated
explicitly, catch rate is catch divided by effort throughout, as in a
real fishery, rather than a latent density.

The fishery independent survey samples a fixed fraction of cells without
replacement in every kth month, by simple random sampling or with equal
allocation across latitude bands, and observes them through its own
delta lognormal model with a survey specific catchability, encounter
scale and coefficient of variation, none of which responds to the event.
Because inclusion probabilities are known and equal, the survey index
and its centre of gravity are computed with design based estimators: a
Horvitz--Thompson total for the index and a Hájek ratio for the
centroid.

\hypertarget{why-a-fishery-independent-survey-is-not-simply-another-catch-rate}{%
\subsection{Why a fishery independent survey is not simply another catch
rate}\label{why-a-fishery-independent-survey-is-not-simply-another-catch-rate}}

A survey also records catch and effort, so it is reasonable to ask how a
survey index differs from the CPUE whose failure this paper establishes.
The arithmetic is indeed the same: both divide catch by effort. What
differs is the statistical provenance of the effort and the
catchability, and it is those two properties, not the ratio itself, that
determine whether the index tracks abundance. Three differences are
decisive, and each corresponds to one term in the operating model.

First, effort in a commercial fishery is allocated preferentially. In
the model, the multinomial allocation weights depend on the catch rate
believed from the previous month, on distance from port, and, during
events, on the contraction term; effort therefore goes where fish are
expected, so its spatial distribution is correlated with the very state
being estimated. A survey allocates effort by design: stations are drawn
with known, equal inclusion probabilities that do not depend on
abundance. The commercial index is a state weighted average over a non
random, self selected footprint, whereas the survey index is a
probability weighted average over a footprint fixed in advance. This is
the standard distinction between a preferential sample and a probability
sample, and it is why the survey total is formed as a Horvitz--Thompson
estimator with known inclusion probabilities rather than as a raw ratio.

Second, commercial catchability responds to the driver. In the model the
uniform component of catchability, the factor that scales with event
severity, is exactly the quantity that is structurally confounded with
abundance through the reparameterisation of Section 2.1: multiplying
abundance by any positive monthly sequence and dividing catchability by
the same sequence leaves every commercial observation unchanged. The
survey holds its gear, its tow, and its protocol fixed, so its
catchability \(q^{\text{surv}}\) is constant and, critically, does not
carry that severity dependent factor. In the notation of the invariance,
the survey observation \(q^{\text{surv}} N_t \pi_{st}\) is not
multiplied by \(a_t\), so it is not invariant under the
reparameterisation and breaks the degeneracy. This is the precise sense
in which the survey catchability must be independent of the confounder:
were a survey to let its own catchability drift with the heatwave, or
were it standardised using the same fleet behaviour that generates the
confounding, its information would remain singular and it would recover
no more than the fishery does.

Third, the fishery samples only where it fishes, and it stops fishing
the warm edge precisely when catchability falls there; the survey
samples the whole frame regardless of local catch rates, which is why
the survey centroid registers genuine redistribution but not a warm edge
catchability change. A survey that shared the fishery's spatial coverage
would inherit the same blind spot.

A fourth point concerns what the survey can and cannot see rather than
how it samples. A survey of the frame estimates in frame abundance. If
an event moves biomass across the frame boundary, in frame and total
abundance diverge, and the survey, like the fishery, has no observation
of the stock beyond the edge. The survey therefore removes the
catchability confound and the effort confound; it does not remove the
frame confound, and no design confined to the frame can. This is why two
estimands are carried (Section 2.3), and why the survey is benchmarked
against in frame abundance, for which it is unbiased, as well as against
total abundance, for which it is not when biomass leaves.

A survey index is therefore not privileged because it is a different
kind of measurement; it is privileged only when its effort is allocated
independently of abundance and its catchability is held constant and
known. Those are design choices, not automatic properties of survey
data, and a poorly designed survey that violated either would be as
confounded as the fishery. The results that follow quantify how much of
a well designed survey is needed: the abundance signal is recovered only
once coverage is adequate (Section 4.4), and at very low coverage the
survey fails through sampling variance rather than through the
structural confounding that defeats the fishery, which is a different
and more tractable kind of failure.

\hypertarget{estimators}{%
\subsection{Estimators}\label{estimators}}

We benchmark a graded sequence of estimators rather than a single index,
so that biases which a better model can remove are separated from
confounds which no model can remove. The nominal index is total catch
over total effort, standardised only for the trend and seasonal terms.
The positive only index is the classical demeaned log CPUE computed on
positive catches alone, discarding zeros, and stands in for the still
common practice that the delta model is meant to replace. The delta two
way index fits an encounter and a positive component, each with additive
cell and month effects, and predicts onto the complete reference grid at
fixed reference effort, so that the index reflects the modelled density
rather than where effort happened to go. The delta index with a spatial
severity interaction adds a low dimensional interaction between position
and event severity to the two way model, giving a standardised density
surface on fixed support that is free to move with the event; this is
required for the centroid diagnostic, because a purely additive model
has a spatial shape that is fixed by construction and cannot move its
own centre of gravity (Thorson et al., 2016a). Because the fleet
contracts away from part of the domain during events, the delta model's
prediction for unfished cells is an imputation; its share is reported,
and a variant that averages the fitted density only over the cells
fished in each month is reported alongside the full grid index so that
the influence of the imputation can be judged. The survey index is the
design based total described above, with a model based counterpart
fitted to the survey observations for comparison.

Every index is reduced to a single heatwave coefficient by the same
regression: the partial slope of the log index on centred event
severity, controlling for a linear time trend and a first order seasonal
harmonic, with a Newey--West standard error on the severity term.
Applying an identical specification to the estimands and to every
estimator guarantees that all coefficients target the same quantities,
and the standard error lets us report, for each estimator, the coverage
of its 95 percent interval and its power to reject a zero effect.

\hypertarget{spatial-and-temporal-diagnostics}{%
\subsection{Spatial and temporal
diagnostics}\label{spatial-and-temporal-diagnostics}}

Two spatial centroids are reported and kept conceptually distinct. The
fleet weighted catch centroid is the catch weighted mean position of the
fishery, a description of where the fleet operated; it is a fishery use
indicator, not an unbiased estimator of the stock's location. The fixed
reference grid centroid is the mean position of the standardised density
surface from the delta interaction model, which is an estimator of the
stock's distribution. Alongside these we compute the survey centroid
from the design based survey. Each centroid is summarised by its event
contrast, the mean poleward position during event months minus the mean
outside them, and compared against the centroid of the latent
distribution, which is known.

The temporal question, whether a contemporaneous specification can
capture a lagged and cumulative decline, is addressed by fitting a
contemporaneous model and a penalised distributed lag model to two
targets. The primary target is the survey index, detrended and
deseasonalised, which is what an analyst can observe; the second is the
true event attributable effect, which bounds what any model of the lag
structure could recover. The distributed lag spans a twenty four month
window covering the memory of the accumulating stock, estimates the lag
weights rather than supplying them, and penalises their second
differences with a smoothing parameter selected by generalised cross
validation. The two models are compared on blocked out of sample
prediction with the smoothing parameter re-selected inside each training
block, so that no information from a held out block enters its choice,
which separates description of the mechanism from short horizon
predictive skill without favouring the more flexible model.

Unless otherwise stated, results are Monte Carlo means over 400
replicates per scenario under common random numbers; the largest Monte
Carlo standard error of any estimator mean in Table 1 is 0.002. Every
comparison is made against the paired counterfactual estimands of
Section 2.3: the event effect on total abundance and on in frame
abundance. The operating model behaves as intended (Figure 1): during a
heatwave the thermal field warms the domain, the stock density contracts
toward the cool poleward edge, and catchability falls at the warm edge,
all emerging from the single thermal driver. The simulated observations
reproduce the features that make CPUE difficult to interpret (Figure 2):
a strongly seasonal temperature field punctuated by events, catch rates
that are zero on roughly half of fished cells and right skewed where
positive, and a fished footprint that fluctuates with the fleet's
beliefs. The dynamics make the confounding concrete (Figure 3): under
each mechanism the observed indices are shown against both truths over

\hypertarget{structural-non-identifiability-and-what-is-identified}{%
\subsection{Structural non identifiability, and what is
identified}\label{structural-non-identifiability-and-what-is-identified}}

The invariance argument of Section 2.1 says that the observed
information for the pair of abundance and uniform catchability slopes is
singular under a fishery only likelihood, and the reduced likelihood of
Section 3.1 confirms it in the way such an illustration can: its
eigenvalues are approximately 1209 and 0, the second zero to numerical
precision, and the profile likelihood in the abundance slope is flat to
within 2e-07 log likelihood units over a range of 0.9 in the slope
(Figure 9c). Adding survey observations with protocol fixed catchability
gives eigenvalues of about 2287 and 387 and a profile spanning 322 log
likelihood units over the same range. We stress that this is a
consequence of the functional form of the observation model rather than
an independent test of it; its value is in making the flat direction
concrete. The informative check is the third parameter. Adding a warm
edge catchability slope that multiplies a cell level weight, the three
parameter fishery only information has eigenvalues 2900, 190, 5e-13:
exactly one flat direction, the uniform one, and a curved profile in the
spatial slope from fishery data alone (Figure 9d). Spatially structured
catchability is identified without a survey; spatially uniform
catchability is not. That distinction organises the rest of the results.

\hypertarget{which-estimators-recover-which-estimand}{%
\subsection{Which estimators recover which
estimand}\label{which-estimators-recover-which-estimand}}

Figure 5 and Table 1 report every estimator's heatwave coefficient
across the sixteen cells of the factorial and the four warm edge cells,
against both estimands, and Figure 4 shows the sampling distributions of
the key quantities across replicates. Three patterns hold across every
cell.

First, under a pure decline every index that keeps its zeros recovers
the effect: with a truth of -0.024 the nominal index reads -0.028, the
delta model -0.028 and the survey -0.024. The positive only index is
erratic rather than merely attenuated, reading -0.007 without the fleet
contraction and -0.028 with it, because the zeros it discards carry the
contraction's signal. A decline that is not accompanied by any change in
catchability or distribution is not hard to see in CPUE; the difficulty
is that heatwaves do not produce such declines.

Second, no fishery index is robust to uniform catchability change, and
nothing in the fishery data can make it so. Under catchability loss with
no change in abundance, the nominal index reads -0.038, the delta model
-0.043 and the delta model with a spatial interaction -0.042, all
reporting a decline that did not occur, while the survey reads -0.001.
When decline and catchability loss coincide, the nominal coefficient
(-0.069) is 3 times the true effect (-0.023) and the delta model is no
better (-0.072); the survey reads -0.026. This is the structural result
of Section 4.1 showing up in finite samples, and it is the same under
every combination of the other factors.

Third, redistribution separates the two estimands, and the estimators
sort themselves by which one they track. Under redistribution with no
change in abundance, the effect on total abundance is zero while the
effect on in frame abundance is -0.010, because an event moves 3 percent
of the stock across the poleward boundary. The survey, which samples the
frame, reads -0.008: it is unbiased for in frame abundance and biased
for total abundance by exactly the frame share. Across all ten cells in
which redistribution is active, the survey's error against in frame
abundance is at most 0.003, within the Monte Carlo standard error, while
its error against total abundance is close to -0.010 in every one of
them. The delta model with a spatial severity interaction, which lets
the standardised density surface move, tracks in frame abundance when
decline and redistribution coincide (-0.025 against -0.025) and recovers
part of it under pure redistribution (-0.004 against -0.010), in both
cases far closer than the fixed shape models; this is a success of
spatially explicit standardisation, and it means the availability
confound, unlike the catchability confound, has a fishery only remedy.
The nominal index does not have one. It reads -0.045 under pure
redistribution, several times the in frame effect, because the fleet's
belief about where fish are lags the stock, and the two way delta model,
whose spatial shape is fixed, reads -0.035.

The fleet's event driven contraction, a factor in its own right in this
design, has small effects. Alone it is harmless (nominal 0.001 against a
truth of zero); it shrinks the fished footprint by 0.022 of the domain
during events, and its interactions with the biological factors are of
order 0.003 (Table 2). Because the fleet contracts away from part of the
domain, the delta model's full grid prediction imputes 71 percent of
cells in event months; the fished cells only variant differs from the
full grid index by at most 0.005 in any cell, so the imputation is not
what drives the delta model's behaviour here. All two way solvers
converged in 100 percent of replicates.

\hypertarget{availability-and-warm-edge-catchability-are-separated-only-by-an-independent-survey}{%
\subsection{Availability and warm edge catchability are separated only
by an independent
survey}\label{availability-and-warm-edge-catchability-are-separated-only-by-an-independent-survey}}

Figure 8 and Table 3 report the centroid diagnostics. Under pure
redistribution the latent centroid shifts poleward by 0.30 grid bands
during events; the fleet weighted fishery centroid recovers 0.26 of
that, the survey centroid 0.28 and the delta surface centroid 0.22. The
scalar coefficient on total abundance is blind to this movement, so the
spatial diagnostic adds real information. The warm edge catchability
cells show why that information is not sufficient on its own. When
catchability declines only at the warm edge, with no change in abundance
or distribution, the fishery centroid shifts poleward by 0.20 bands,
imitating a genuine redistribution, because the warm edge cells stop
contributing catch and the catch weighted centre of mass moves away from
them; the survey centroid moves by 0.00. The centre of gravity of an
independent survey is therefore the diagnostic that separates true
movement from a warm edge catchability artefact; the fishery centroid
alone cannot make that separation, and neither can the fishery index,
which reads -0.107 against a truth of 0.001.

\hypertarget{what-a-survey-must-look-like-to-recover-the-effect}{%
\subsection{What a survey must look like to recover the
effect}\label{what-a-survey-must-look-like-to-recover-the-effect}}

Figure 6 and Table 4 report the survey design curve for the decline plus
uniform catchability cell with the fleet contraction active, the case in
which the fishery is most badly misled. The abundance estimand here is a
real decline that the fishery indices overstate: the nominal and delta
indices sit at a root mean squared error of 0.058 and 0.065 against the
true effect regardless of any survey, and their 95 percent intervals
cover the truth in 55 percent and 54 percent of replicates: they are
confidently wrong. A monthly survey of 7 stations (84 tows a year)
already has a lower error (0.043) than either fishery index, 14 stations
bring it to 0.031 with interval coverage of 87 percent, and the curve
flattens beyond that. At three stations a month the survey is still
competitive on median error but unstable in its tails, because a month
in which no station catches anything is common (2.53 such months per
series on average) and the log index then carries no information.

Frequency matters more than station count. A quarterly survey needs 29
stations per survey (116 tows a year) merely to match the fishery's
error (0.091), its error is dominated by occasional large misses (median
absolute error 0.026 against a root mean squared error two to four times
larger at every station count), and its interval coverage falls to 81
percent because thirty two observations over eight years leave little
room for the trend and seasonal controls. A semiannual survey cannot
separate the seasonal sine and cosine at all, since it samples the same
two months every year; the regression drops one of them, the standard
error is no longer trustworthy (coverage 60 percent), and the apparent
power of that design in Figure 6b is an artefact of undercoverage, which
is why those points are drawn open. An annual survey is not estimable
for an event scale effect within an eight year series. Stratifying the
monthly survey by latitude band does not change the design curve at this
coverage (Table 4), because the inclusion probabilities are the same and
the between band variance is small relative to the within band variance
of a delta lognormal catch.

Power is the sobering column. At 14 stations a month the survey rejects
a zero effect in 24 percent of replicates for a true decline of -0.023
per standard deviation of severity, rising only to 35 percent at 58
stations: the effect is small relative to the month to month variability
of a catch rate, and station count buys precision slowly once the survey
is no longer sparse. The fishery indices reject in 75 percent of
replicates, but that is the power to detect a biased effect, not the
true one. The survey is the only index that is right; it is right with
wide intervals.

\hypertarget{frame-geometry-and-the-sign-of-the-redistribution-artefact}{%
\subsection{Frame geometry and the sign of the redistribution
artefact}\label{frame-geometry-and-the-sign-of-the-redistribution-artefact}}

An earlier version of this framework reported that redistribution turns
the CPUE coefficient positive when the frame is closed and negative when
it is open. Neither half of that claim survives the corrected model, and
the correction is instructive (Figure 10, Table 6). Under a refuge that
experiences the same ocean as the frame, an event moves 3 percent of the
stock beyond the poleward edge, the in frame effect is -0.010, and the
survey reads -0.010. Under a refuge with fixed near optimal suitability,
the specification of the earlier version, the sign of the frame share
change reverses: the heatwave warms the cool northern frame toward the
thermal optimum, the frame becomes more attractive than a refuge that
never warms, and 3 percent of the stock moves \emph{into} the frame
during events, so the in frame effect is 0.007 and the survey reads
0.008. The earlier version's positive survey coefficient under
redistribution, which it described as ``centred on zero'', was this
influx. A refuge that does not warm is a thermostat, and the direction
in which it moves biomass is an artefact of the specification rather
than a property of heatwaves.

The nominal index, by contrast, falls under redistribution in every
frame setting: -0.036 closed, -0.045 open with a dynamic refuge, -0.028
open with a static one. The sign of the redistribution artefact in CPUE
is set by whether the fleet tracks the stock faster than the stock
moves, not by the geometry of the frame; here the fleet's belief lags
the stock, so catch rates fall where the fleet keeps fishing. The
positive hyperstable coefficient of the classical literature requires a
fleet that follows the stock into its contracted range; consistently,
the sensitivity analysis of Section 4.7 shows the nominal artefact
growing with the stock's movement rate and shrinking when the fleet
contracts onto the cells it knows best, while the fleet's learning rate
on its own has little effect. The practical lesson is that the sign of a
redistribution artefact is not universal, and that an attribution
exercise must consider the fleet's behaviour relative to the stock's
movement rather than assuming hyperstability.

\hypertarget{a-distributed-lag-describes-the-lagged-response-it-does-not-predict-better-out-of-sample}{%
\subsection{A distributed lag describes the lagged response; it does not
predict better out of
sample}\label{a-distributed-lag-describes-the-lagged-response-it-does-not-predict-better-out-of-sample}}

The temporal experiment gives the operating model a genuine three month
lag between thermal stress and the abundance response, with slow
recovery so that the effect is cumulative and persistent, and fits a
contemporaneous model and a penalised distributed lag model to two
targets on a common centred scale (Table 7). The primary target is the
survey index with trend and season removed, which is what an analyst can
observe. There, the distributed lag raises the coefficient of
determination from 0.02 to 0.19 and its fitted series correlates with
the true event effect at 0.24 against 0.09 for the contemporaneous
model, with about 7 effective degrees of freedom in the lag weights; the
recovered weights (Figure 7b) have their mass spread over the window,
the delayed and cumulative shape of the true response. Fitted to the
true effect itself, which bounds what any such model could recover, the
distributed lag reaches a correlation of 0.51 against 0.14. A
contemporaneous model is structurally unable to represent a lagged,
accumulating response, and the experiment bears this out.

The caution is equally clear. On blocked out of sample prediction, with
the smoothing parameter re-selected inside each training block so that
no information leaks from the held out block, the distributed lag does
not beat the contemporaneous model on either target (root mean squared
error 0.55 against 0.44 on the survey index), because its additional
parameters add variance that offsets the reduction in bias. The
distributed lag is the better description of the mechanism; it is not
the better short horizon predictor, and the two criteria should not be
conflated.

\hypertarget{global-sensitivity}{%
\subsection{Global sensitivity}\label{global-sensitivity}}

A space filling design over fifteen parameters that are otherwise held
fixed, evaluated at 200 design points with 20 replicates each,
identifies which of them govern the confounds (Figure 9e, f; Table 8).
The bias of the nominal index is governed by four parameters of
comparable weight: it shrinks when the fleet contracts onto its best
known cells during events (standardised regression coefficient 0.00),
and grows with the stock's movement rate (-0.12), the magnitude of
catchability loss (-0.58) and the number of events (-0.31), with a
design level coefficient of determination of 0.53; the fleet's learning
rate matters little (0.07). The survey's error against in frame
abundance is governed by its own design, survey coverage (-0.44) and
encounter scale (-0.36), and by the size of the effect being estimated
(0.33), while its bias against total abundance is governed by the
movement rate (-0.38), the number of events and niche breadth, the
parameters that set how much biomass crosses the frame boundary, and not
by anything about the fishery. The qualitative conclusions are therefore
stable across a wide region of parameter space.

\hypertarget{a-diagnostic-key-and-its-error-rate}{%
\subsection{A diagnostic key and its error
rate}\label{a-diagnostic-key-and-its-error-rate}}

The mechanisms leave a joint signature across the estimators: a decline
lowers both the delta and the survey index; redistribution moves the
fishery and survey centroids together and lowers the survey index by the
frame share; uniform catchability lowers the fishery indices but not the
survey; warm edge catchability moves the fishery centroid but not the
survey centroid. Treating this as a classification problem, we learn
decision thresholds on a training half of the replicates and evaluate
them on a held out half. The resulting key assigns the correct mechanism
to 51 percent of held out replicates across six classes, against a
chance rate near 17 percent, and returns an indeterminate verdict on 11
percent (Table 5). The confusion structure is informative in its own
right: redistribution and warm edge catchability are recovered reliably,
whereas abundance decline and uniform catchability are the pair most
often confused with each other, exactly as the identifiability analysis
predicts. The key is thus useful but explicitly probabilistic, and its
principal value is to make the residual ambiguity between abundance and
catchability quantitative rather than to resolve it.

\hypertarget{discussion}{%
\section{Discussion}\label{discussion}}

The central result is a negative one made precise and then made useful.
Nominal and even well standardised CPUE indices cannot, as a matter of
the likelihood, separate a heatwave's effect on abundance from its
effect on uniform catchability, because the two occupy a flat direction
of the fishery likelihood. This is not a bias to be bounded; it is an
identification failure that follows from the form of the observation
model, and the finite sample experiments show it operating in every cell
of the design. The difficulty is more than theoretical. A recent
analysis of 248 marine heatwaves across North American and European
continental shelves found that their effects on demersal fish biomass
were frequently indistinguishable from natural and sampling variability,
and were not consistently associated with community reorganisation
(Fredston et al., 2023). That result, obtained from fishery independent
trawl surveys, sharpens the motivation for the present work: if a
heatwave signal is hard to detect even in survey data collected under a
fixed design, then reading it from commercial catch rates, where
abundance is entangled with availability and catchability, is harder
still. Our power results make the same point from the other side: a
survey that is unbiased for the abundance effect still rejects a zero
effect in only a minority of replicates at the station counts that make
it better than the fishery.

The constructive half of the framework is a mapping from confounding
pathway to remedy, and the revised model makes that mapping sharper than
the earlier version did. Availability change has a fishery only remedy:
a spatially explicit standardisation whose density surface can move with
the event recovers in frame abundance under redistribution, and the
centre of gravity of that surface recovers the movement itself. Uniform
catchability change has no fishery only remedy at all, because no
covariate of the fishing operation observes it, and this is precisely
the direction that a survey is needed to pin down. Spatially structured
catchability sits between the two: it is identified from the fishery
data, but it imitates redistribution in the fishery's own centre of
gravity, and only the centre of gravity of an independent survey tells
the two apart. That availability itself moves in complex ways during
heatwaves is now well documented: modelling of fourteen top predators
across four Northeast Pacific heatwaves found responses ranging from
near total loss of habitat to a two fold increase, varying by species
and by event (Welch et al., 2023), so the sign and magnitude of an
availability driven CPUE signal cannot be assumed a priori. The bias
induced by non random, preferential allocation of commercial effort is
itself an active area of study: simulation work shows that fishery
dependent sampling in proportion to biomass can, under favourable
conditions, recover indices comparable to random sampling, but degrades
once the spatial mismatch between effort and abundance becomes severe
(Ducharme-Barth et al., 2022), and the difficulty of projecting
distributions from such data has motivated dedicated methods (Karp et
al., 2023). Our results are consistent with that literature and add the
identifiability perspective.

The survey deserves a more careful statement than ``it recovers
abundance''. It bears emphasis that the survey helps not because it
measures something other than catch per effort, but because its effort
is allocated independently of abundance and its catchability is held
fixed and known; a survey that sampled preferentially or let its
catchability track the heatwave would be confounded in the same way as
the fishery and would not restore identifiability. What a well designed
survey of the frame recovers is in frame abundance. When an event moves
biomass across the frame boundary, in frame and total abundance diverge
by the frame share, and the survey, like the fishery, has no observation
of the stock beyond the edge. Whether that matters depends on the
management question: if the stock beyond the boundary is lost to the
fishery and its management, in frame abundance is the right target and
the survey is unbiased for it; if the boundary is an artefact of
jurisdiction and the stock is expected to return, total abundance is the
target and the survey will overstate a decline by the share that left.
The earlier version of this work conflated the two, and the direction of
its error was set by a refuge that never warmed. The practical
recommendation is to decide which estimand a survey is meant to serve
before it is designed, and to extend its frame beyond the historical
range of the stock where the second question is the one being asked.

A further reason the confound matters is that the two processes it
entangles are themselves correlated in nature, not independent.
Redistribution and abundance decline are widely treated as alternative
interpretations of a falling local catch rate, one benign and one not,
yet a global analysis of 2,572 marine fish population trends found that
rapid poleward range shifts tend to coincide with local population
declines rather than with stable abundance, so that a shifting stock is
often also a declining one (Chaikin et al., 2024). If redistribution and
decline travel together, then a CPUE change that appears to be ``just''
a distributional artefact may signal genuine loss, and the inability of
a scalar index to tell them apart is not a conservative bias but a
potentially dangerous one. This strengthens the case for the survey
based diagnostics developed here, which estimate the two processes
separately rather than forcing an analyst to guess which one a catch
rate reflects.

Two results run against expectations that the earlier version of this
framework shared with much of the literature, and both are worth stating
plainly. The first is that redistribution lowered nominal CPUE in every
frame setting, including a closed frame in which biomass merely
concentrates. The classical hyperstability signature, a catch rate that
holds up while the stock contracts, requires a fleet that follows the
stock into its contracted range; the fleet modelled here learns from
last month's catch rates and so lags a stock that moves within a season,
and the sensitivity analysis shows the artefact growing with the stock's
movement rate and shrinking when the fleet contracts onto the cells it
knows best. Hyperstability is a property of the fleet's response to the
stock, not of redistribution as such, and an attribution exercise should
characterise that response rather than assume either sign. The second is
that an estimator which shares a month effect between fishery and survey
observations, the natural form of an integrated index, loads the
fishery's catchability change onto the shared effect and recovers only
part of a decline; we fitted such an estimator in an earlier version and
do not report it, because an integrated index helps only if the stream
specific time effects are constrained in a way that the identifiability
result shows cannot be learned from the data.

Several limitations bound the claims. The operating model is a
deliberately simplified world; its lattice, its single environmental
driver and its thermal niche formulation are caricatures of a real
fishery, chosen so that the truth is known rather than so that any
particular stock is reproduced. The structural identifiability result is
general, because it follows from the form of the observation model
rather than from any parameter value, but the finite sample magnitudes,
the station counts and frequencies, the specific error rates of the
diagnostic key, are properties of this parameterisation and should be
read as illustrative orders of magnitude rather than transferable
constants. Catch does not deplete a cell locally, so hyperstability
through the sequential depletion of aggregations cannot arise here; the
two way delta model with cell and month fixed effects is an incidental
parameters setting whose behaviour at this sparsity we have
characterised empirically rather than theoretically; and the Newey--West
intervals undercover somewhat at monthly frequency and badly at
semiannual frequency, which we report rather than correct. The survey is
idealised with known, equal inclusion probabilities and protocol fixed
catchability; real surveys have their own catchability and design
complications, which would raise the coverage required. And we have
treated the four mechanisms as separable switches, whereas in a real
heatwave they are driven by a common process and may be correlated in
ways the factorial does not capture, as the co-occurrence of shift and
decline noted above illustrates. Each of these is a direction for
extension rather than a threat to the core argument, which rests on the
identifiability structure and not on the details of the world.

The framework applies directly to any fishery where a heatwave signal is
being read from catch rates, and the natural next step is to instantiate
it for a specific stock. Western Australian fisheries that experienced
the 2010 to 2011 marine heatwave, driven by an anomalously strong
Leeuwin Current during an extreme La Niña (Pearce and Feng, 2013; Feng
et al., 2013), and Spanish mackerel in particular, offer a setting in
which the operating model could be conditioned on real spatial
structure, real effort and a real survey, turning the qualitative design
guidance here into stock specific monitoring recommendations. That
heatwave had documented and lasting consequences for the region's fished
stocks, and early detection through fishery independent surveys was
central to the management response (Caputi et al., 2016, 2019), which is
exactly the kind of auxiliary information this framework identifies as
necessary. That application is the subject of a companion study.

\hypertarget{conclusion}{%
\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}}

Catch per unit effort cannot, on its own, tell whether a marine heatwave
made a stock decline, move or merely become harder to catch, and this is
a property of the likelihood rather than a shortcoming of any
standardisation. The way out is not a better model of the catch rate but
a different kind of observation. A fishery independent survey with
design allocated effort and protocol fixed catchability restores
identifiability, is unbiased for the abundance inside its frame, and
separates genuine movement from a warm edge catchability change through
its centre of gravity; a spatially explicit standardisation of the
fishery data recovers in frame abundance under redistribution but not
under catchability change; and no index confined to the frame can see
the share of a stock that an event moves beyond it. How much survey is
needed is a quantitative question with an answer that depends on
frequency more than on station count. For a management agency facing the
next heatwave, the implication is that the data infrastructure to
attribute its effect must be in place before the event, and that its
frame, its frequency and the estimand it serves are design decisions
that this framework can inform.

\hypertarget{data-and-code-availability}{%
\section*{Data and code availability}\label{data-and-code-availability}}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Data and code availability}

All code required to reproduce the operating model, the estimators,
every experiment, every table and figure, and the manuscript itself is
provided in a public repository, entirely in R. The complete analysis is
reproduced by a single driver script with stage level caching; a quick
mode reproduces the full pipeline at reduced replication for checking,
and a test suite of forty checks covers the properties on which the
results depend, including that observations are drawn from the state the
estimand is defined on, that fishing mortality is non negative, that the
fleet's contraction changes the allocation, and that the survey is
unbiased for in frame abundance. Every number quoted in the text is
evaluated from the results files when the manuscript is built.
