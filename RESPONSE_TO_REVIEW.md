# Response to review

We thank the reviewer for an unusually thorough and constructive report. The central diagnosis was correct: the manuscript claimed structural identifiability while demonstrating only estimator failure, the observation model was internally inconsistent, and several results were asserted rather than shown. We have rebuilt the analysis to address every priority item. The operating model, estimators, and all experiments were reimplemented and rerun; the manuscript was rewritten around the new results; and the code repository now carries parallel R and Python implementations with a test suite. Two of our original headline findings changed under the corrected model, and we have rewritten them rather than defend the previous versions. What follows maps each priority item to the specific change.

Where we quote a number below, it is from the rerun at 200 Monte Carlo replicates per scenario cell under common random numbers, and the corresponding figure or table is named.

## Critical items

**P1 — Structural versus practical identifiability.** We now prove the structural result rather than infer it from estimator behaviour. Section 2.1 states the invariance directly: every fishery observation depends on the state only through the product of catchability, effort, abundance, and distribution share, so the reparameterisation that scales abundance by any positive monthly sequence and catchability by its inverse leaves the entire fishery likelihood unchanged. Section 4.1 confirms this numerically. The observed Fisher information for the abundance and catchability slopes has eigenvalues of approximately 1096 and 0 under fishery only data, with a profile likelihood in the abundance slope that is exactly flat; adding a survey with independent catchability gives eigenvalues near 2005 and 342, a condition number of about six, and a sharply curved profile. We use "structural non identifiability" only for this likelihood level result and keep "practical" for the finite sample experiments throughout. Figure 2 is new.

**P2 — Inconsistent delta lognormal expectation.** The observation model is reparameterised to be mean coherent. The positive component is now centred so that the encounter probability times the conditional mean returns the latent expected catch rate, so the unconditional mean of the observation equals that rate exactly (Section 3.2). A unit test verifies this on an isolated cell to within Monte Carlo tolerance. The earlier formulation, in which the implied catchability multiplier could exceed one, is gone.

**P2 (estimator suite) — A genuine delta estimator and comparators.** We now benchmark a graded sequence rather than a single index (Section 3.3): nominal CPUE; the positive only demeaned index, retained only as the naive comparator the delta model is meant to replace; a two way fixed effect delta model predicted onto a fixed reference grid; a delta model with a spatial severity interaction that yields a movable density surface; a design based survey index with a model based counterpart; and an integrated fishery plus survey index. Table 1 and Figure 3 report all of them. The key result is now sharper: under a pure decline the delta model recovers the abundance effect almost exactly (−0.026 against a truth of −0.024) whereas the positive only index attenuates it fivefold, showing that the sampling artefact is a bias a better model removes, while under catchability or availability change no fishery index recovers abundance and only the survey does.

**P7 — Counterfactual estimand.** The target is now a paired counterfactual, exactly as the reviewer suggested (Section 2.3). Each replicate is generated twice under common random numbers, once with the event series and once with the event anomaly deleted while climatology, the warming trend, the noise field, the process noise, and the realised effort are held fixed; the difference of the two log abundance trajectories is the event attributable effect, and its regression on severity is the estimand. The identical regression specification, including trend and seasonal controls, is applied to the estimand and to every estimator, so all coefficients target the same quantity.

**P9 — Complete factorial and comparable performance metrics.** All eight cells of the mechanism factorial are now run and reported, together with a sampling only cell and two spatially structured catchability cells (Figure 3, Table 1). Performance is summarised against the common estimand as bias, standard deviation, and root mean squared error with Monte Carlo standard errors, and the survey design comparison (Figure 6, Table 4) reports errors of the survey and fishery estimators against the same estimand rather than against different targets. The log transform and Jensen concern is handled by predicting on the response scale with the standard lognormal bias correction before taking logs for the index.

## Major items

**Interactions rather than assumed additivity (2.2).** We no longer assert that the channels combine additively. A three way analysis of variance of each estimator's coefficient over the factorial (Table 2) reports the main effects and all interactions. The interactions are an order of magnitude smaller than the main effects and mostly within two Monte Carlo standard errors of zero, so the mechanisms are close to but not exactly additive on the coefficient scale, and we state it that way.

**P13 — Sensitivity before the stress test, and a validated classifier.** The global sensitivity analysis now precedes the diagnostic key in the logic of the results. It uses a 160 point space filling design over twelve previously fixed parameters, summarised by standardised regression coefficients (Figure 7); nominal bias is driven chiefly by the magnitude of catchability loss, the event count, and the movement rate. The diagnostic key is now a classifier with thresholds learned on a training half of the replicates and evaluated on a held out half (Table 5): accuracy is about 49 percent across seven classes against a chance rate near 14 percent, with 9 percent indeterminate, and abundance decline versus uniform catchability is the pair most often confused, as the identifiability analysis predicts. We present the key as explicitly probabilistic.

**Warm edge catchability versus redistribution (P9 continuation).** We agree the survey centroid claim was overstated in the original. The revised centroid analysis (Figure 5, Table 3) shows that under genuine redistribution every stream detects the poleward shift, but under a warm edge catchability change with no movement the fishery centroid shifts spuriously by about 0.19 bands while the survey centroid stays at the truth of zero. The independent survey centroid, not the fishery centroid, is therefore the diagnostic that separates the two, which we now state as the recommendation.

**P15 — Sensitivity figure and numbering.** The sensitivity figure is corrected and now shows standardised regression coefficients with standard errors for three response summaries. All figures were regenerated on a colour blind safe palette with consistent colour semantics across panels, shared legends, and captions carried in the text rather than embedded in the plots.

## Two findings that changed

We flag two places where the corrected model overturned an original claim.

First, redistribution no longer produces a positive, hyperstable CPUE coefficient in general. With an open sampling frame, biomass that redistributes toward and beyond the poleward edge partly leaves the fished area, so CPUE falls rather than rises; the positive coefficient appears only in a closed frame where the stock concentrates within the domain (Section 4.5). The sign of a redistribution artefact is therefore set by the geometry of the fishery relative to the movement, and we report both cases rather than assuming hyperstability.

Second, we corrected the temporal analysis. The original comparison fitted to an observed index on one scale and compared it against latent abundance on another, which introduced a spurious level offset, and the operating model as first configured had no genuine lag for a distributed lag model to recover. We rebuilt the experiment with a real three month lag and slow recovery, and fitted both a contemporaneous and a penalised distributed lag model to the same target on the same scale, the true event attributable abundance effect. The distributed lag now clearly captures the lagged, cumulative decline that the contemporaneous model cannot represent, tracking the true trajectory at a correlation near 0.76 against about 0.15 for the contemporaneous model and recovering most of the amplitude the latter misses (Section 4.6, Figure 7). We retain one honest caution: the distributed lag does not beat the contemporaneous model on blocked out of sample prediction, because its extra parameters add variance, so it is the better description of the mechanism rather than necessarily the better short horizon predictor.

## Mathematical formulation

Following the request to strengthen the mathematical representation, the revision now includes a formal statement of the operating model and estimators as a dedicated methods subsection (Section 3.1, Model equations), rendered with the equation editor. It gives the temperature decomposition, the severity definition, the Gaussian thermal niche, the partial adjustment movement with the outside frame state, the abundance state transition and occupancy weighted thermal dose, the uniform and warm edge catchability terms, the coherent delta lognormal observation model with the mean coherence identity, the multinomial effort allocation, the design based survey estimators, the structural non identifiability invariance with the likelihood equality it implies, the paired counterfactual estimand, and each index estimator. The prose subsections that follow describe the same components and give the parameter values.

We also corrected a figure issue the review process surfaced: the spatial snapshot in Figure 1 previously showed a spatially uniform catchability anomaly, because the illustrated scenario activated only the uniform catchability term. Figure 1 now uses a scenario that also activates the warm edge term, so the catchability panel shows the realistic latitudinal gradient the mechanism produces.


## Expanded scholarship

We have broadened the literature base from fourteen to twenty six references, adding work that situates the study more fully in its field. These include the foundational statement that catch per unit effort is difficult to interpret in isolation (Maunder et al., 2006); the spatio-temporal CPUE standardisation literature the framework builds on and departs from (Thorson and Barnett, 2017; Maunder et al., 2020; Thorson et al., 2020); the model based centre of gravity and effective area methods that motivate the spatial diagnostics (Thorson et al., 2016a, 2016b); and the Western Australian marine heatwave and its documented fisheries consequences, which ground the companion study (Wernberg et al., 2013; Pearce and Feng, 2013; Feng et al., 2013; Caputi et al., 2016, 2019; Babcock et al., 2019). Every added citation was checked against the primary source for author, year, journal, volume and pages.

## Structure, references, and reproducibility

We reordered the results so that the structural identifiability proof comes first, the estimator benchmark second, the spatial and survey design results third, and the temporal and sensitivity analyses last, matching the recommended flow. We added the references the reviewer suggested, including the good practice guidance on CPUE standardisation (Hoyle et al., 2024) and the simulation work on sampling design and spatial dependence (Fuster-Alonso et al., 2024), and corrected the incomplete citation of our own related paper, now given with its article number (Afrifa-Yamoah et al., 2026, Reviews in Fish Biology and Fisheries 36:51). The full analysis is reproducible from a single driver script, with a resumable stage runner for the long runs, a parallel Python implementation as an independent cross check, and a test suite that pins the coherence, identifiability, and estimator ordering properties the argument depends on.

# Version 2 (October 2026): changes following a code level review

A review of the repository against the manuscript (CRITICAL_REVIEW_sim-rec.md) found defects in the simulation that affected headline claims. Every item below was fixed, the simulation was rerun at larger sizes, and the manuscript was rewritten from the new results. The repository is now entirely R.

## Defects corrected

1. **Observations were generated from a different abundance than the truth.** Catch was drawn from a pre mortality trajectory while the estimand used the post mortality one. The factual world is now simulated in one sequential loop: effort from the lagged belief, removals from that effort, catch from that abundance.
2. **Fishing mortality could be negative.** It was a z score of the fished footprint. It is now proportional to the expected catch per unit abundance implied by the allocation, so it is non negative and falls when catchability falls.
3. **The fleet contraction did nothing.** It entered the allocation score as a constant added to every cell, which cancels in the softmax. It now sharpens the fleet's targeting during events and shrinks the footprint, and it is a factor of the design (2⁴ rather than 2³).
4. **The declared estimand was not the one used.** Tables, figures, the design curve, the matched design and the sensitivity all compared against the marginal association of factual abundance. Every comparison now uses the paired counterfactual, and both the total and the in frame estimand are reported.
5. **The refuge beyond the frame never warmed.** It drew biomass into the frame during events, producing a positive survey coefficient under redistribution that the text described as centred on zero. The refuge now experiences the same season, trend and anomaly as the frame; the old specification is retained as a comparison (Section 4.5).
6. **The identifiability numerics were tautological.** The reduced likelihood was written in the difference of the two slopes. It is now described as an illustration, and a third parameter shows that spatially structured catchability is identified while uniform catchability is not.
7. **The integrated estimator was mis-specified** (shared month effect, positive only, survey weighted by triplicating rows). It is removed and the reason is stated.
8. **Observation variance did not scale with effort.** Catch is now tow level: encounter and variance depend on effort, with mean coherence at any effort.
9. **Two way logit fixed effects could not converge** under complete separation. A light ridge on the cell effects is used; convergence is recorded and reported.
10. **The distributed lag smoothing parameter leaked into the out of sample comparison.** It is now re-selected inside each training block; the primary target is the observed survey index.
11. **No standard errors.** The heatwave slope now carries a Newey–West standard error; interval coverage and power are reported.
12. **The survey design was monthly only.** Frequency (monthly, quarterly, semiannual) and stratification are part of the design curve.

## Claims that changed

- The survey is unbiased for in frame abundance, not for total abundance, whenever an event moves biomass across the frame boundary. "Only the survey recovers the abundance signal" is replaced by this two estimand statement.
- Redistribution lowers nominal CPUE in every frame setting, including a closed frame; the sign of the artefact is set by the fleet's learning rate relative to the stock's movement, not by frame closure.
- A spatially explicit delta model recovers in frame abundance under redistribution: availability has a fishery only remedy, catchability does not.
- Survey frequency matters more than station count; a semiannual survey cannot separate the event from the seasonal cycle within an eight year series.

## Reproducibility

One command (`make run`) regenerates every result with stage caching; `make tables`, `make figures` and `make doc` regenerate the tables, the figures and the manuscript, in which every quoted number is evaluated from the results files. A test suite of forty checks includes tests that would have caught defects 1, 2, 3 and 5. The Python port and the Node document pipeline were removed.
